# ABILITY-FIRST GROWTH STRATEGY

## purpose
Define the default growth philosophy for `SEAL_HAT_LLM` using an ability-first, DEN-inspired approach.

In this repository, the primary question is no longer:
- how many parameters does the model have?

The primary question becomes:
- what abilities does it have?
- which abilities are missing?
- what governed structural change is justified to acquire the missing ability without damaging the old ones?

This document treats parameter count as a capacity constraint, not the main identity of the system.

---

## core idea
The system should grow around **abilities**, not vanity size.

That means the model family is evaluated by whether it can stably express abilities such as:
- language fluency
- slot awareness
- harness compliance
- memory-first behavior
- escalation discipline
- tool-use competence
- multimodal grounding
- specialist lane depth
- reflection and postmortem quality

A larger model is not automatically more mature.
A model is more mature when it demonstrates stable governed ability.

---

## DEN-style interpretation for MM-ELLS
The original spirit of DEN is useful here:
- selectively retrain when needed
- expand only when a persistent new demand appears
- protect existing knowledge from being overwritten
- later prune or compress what did not prove useful

For `SEAL_HAT_LLM`, this becomes:
- stable constitutional core
- expandable ability modules
- parent-approved growth triggers
- harness-verified evidence before permanent growth
- pruning/compression of weak or redundant additions

The repository should interpret DEN as **governed expansion**, not uncontrolled mutation.

---

## three classes of abilities

### 1. constitutional abilities
These should remain close to the stable self of the lineage:
- language coherence
- identity continuity
- slot reading
- harness obedience
- governance refusal
- memory-first instinct
- honest escalation

These are the most expensive abilities to damage and the least acceptable to let drift casually.

### 2. operational abilities
These may improve through bounded growth:
- summarization quality
- retrieval usage
- tool planning
- postmortem quality
- evidence synthesis
- multimodal handling
- recovery behavior

These should be allowed to improve, but still through governed channels.

### 3. specialist abilities
These often belong outside the constitutional root:
- OCR/layout understanding
- audio transcription
- video interpretation
- multimodal fusion
- engineering depth
- legal evidence structuring
- scientific computation procedures

These abilities should usually live in specialists, expert banks, adapters, or governed branches rather than constantly reshaping the whole root.

---

## growth trigger policy
A DEN-style expansion should happen only when all of the following are true:
1. the ability gap is real
2. the gap persists across evals and not just one failure
3. memory/retrieval alone does not solve it
4. routing alone does not solve it
5. a small adapter or prompt overlay alone does not solve it
6. the new structure is justified by repeated operational value

Growth should not happen because:
- one task was hard
- the user asks for more intelligence in the abstract
- the system feels immature without evidence
- a model is compared to a larger benchmark and seems smaller

---

## preferred growth surfaces
When a new ability is needed, prefer these growth surfaces in order:

1. **memory / retrieval improvement**
2. **prompt / skill / tool policy refinement**
3. **adapter or LoRA family**
4. **new expert bank or routed branch**
5. **new specialist descendant**
6. **rare root-level architectural change**

The constitutional root should be the last place that gets structurally altered, not the first.

---

## ability ledger
Every important model or specialist should maintain an ability ledger.

Each tracked ability should have:
- name
- current maturity stage
- evidence source
- eval score or qualitative assessment
- known failure patterns
- last improvement action
- next candidate action

Example:
- `language_coherence` → stable
- `slot_awareness` → stable
- `harness_compliance` → stable
- `multimodal_grounding` → immature
- `video_understanding` → absent
- `tool_planning` → improving
- `postmortem_quality` → adolescent

This gives the harness and parent something concrete to govern.

---

## ability maturity stages
Abilities should mature in stages just like the developmental model.

### infancy
- ability is mostly absent or reflexive
- high supervision
- low trust

### childhood
- ability appears, but is inconsistent
- repeated correction helps
- early reliability signals form

### adolescence
- ability is useful but uneven under stress
- failure patterns become important
- evals should begin gating trust

### adulthood
- ability is stable and trusted within lane
- failure modes are understood
- escalation remains healthy

The model can be mature in one ability and immature in another.
That is expected.

---

## identity continuity rule
Abilities should grow modularly while identity stays continuous.

That means:
- the self is not just the current parameter count
- the self is not just the currently active expert set
- the self is preserved through slots, memory, postmortems, eval history, and harness-governed continuity

This lets the system become more capable without losing who it is.

---

## pruning and compression
If new ability modules are added and later prove weak, redundant, or obsolete, they may be:
- pruned
- compressed
- retired
- merged into more stable descendants

Pruning should be governed by evidence, not aesthetics.
Prune what is not earning its keep.
Do not prune away constitutional continuity casually.

---

## summary
The default DEN-style strategy for `SEAL_HAT_LLM` is:
- focus on abilities first
- treat size as a capacity constraint, not identity
- expand only when a real ability gap persists
- prefer modular growth over root mutation
- use the harness and parent to govern expansion and pruning
- track maturity by ability, not just by parameter count

A model in this system does not merely become bigger.
It becomes more able.
